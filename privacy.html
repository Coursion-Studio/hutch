Privacy Policy

Last updated 16 October 2026

Hutch has no accounts and never builds a profile of you. Everything it indexes — your apps, files, clipboard, windows and running processes — stays on your Mac. It does send two kinds of anonymous telemetry, crash reports and usage counts. Both are on by default, the welcome tour asks about them openly, and each has its own switch. What follows is every time the app goes online, and the rest are things you either start yourself or that happen when you buy.

What the app does on your Mac

Everything Hutch indexes — your apps, files, clipboard history, windows, running processes — stays on your Mac. None of it is transmitted anywhere. Your clipboard history is stored locally and never leaves the machine.

Every time the app talks to a server

This is the complete list. Anything not on it happens on your Mac.

  • Update checks. Once an hour, unless you switch automatic checks off, Hutch fetches a small feed from hutch.coursion.studio. Installing an update downloads it from GitHub. These requests reveal only what any download does: your IP address and app version. No system profile is sent.
  • Crash reports and usage counts. Sent to Sentry and PostHog, both in the EU, while their switches are on. Exactly what they contain is in the section below.
  • Licence activation. If you buy Hutch, activating a Mac sends us your licence key, a device ID, and the Mac's name and model, so the licence page can show which Macs a key is on. The device ID is a random number Hutch makes up on first use, not a hardware serial. Removing a licence or freeing a seat sends the key and that ID again.
  • Licence check. While a Mac is licensed, Hutch asks at most once a day whether the key is still valid, sending the key and the device ID. If the server can't be reached, nothing changes.
  • Currency rates. Only when you convert a currency in Pounce, at most every 12 hours: a plain download of public exchange rates from jsDelivr (fallback: Cloudflare Pages). Nothing about you is sent.
  • Your public IP. Only when you press Return on “Public IP” in Pounce's network info, Hutch asks api.ipify.org.
  • AI features. Only if you enter your own API key. Those requests go straight from your Mac to that provider. We never see them and never proxy them. Your key is stored in your Mac's Keychain.
  • Images dropped on Stash. Dragging an image from a web page onto a shelf downloads that image from its own site, as a browser would.

Web searches, links, checkout and the App Store pages open in your browser; Hutch hands them over and makes no request of its own. Bug reports open in your own mail client with the full text visible, and nothing is sent until you press Send there. iCloud sync writes a file to your iCloud Drive, which Apple syncs; we run no server for it.

What we store when you buy

A purchase creates one record: your email address, the order id, how many seats you bought, when it was issued, which Macs have activated, and what the sale was worth — the amount, currency, and the country our payment reseller reports for tax. We do not store your licence key itself — we can regenerate it when you ask, which means a copy of our database is not a pile of usable keys.

We never see your card details. Payment is handled by our reseller — Paddle or Creem, whichever your receipt names — who act as Merchant of Record. Each has its own privacy policy covering the payment.

This website

We count pageviews so we know whether anyone is reading. There are no cookies and no local storage, and nothing follows you to other sites.

For each visit we record the page path and, if there is one, the domain you arrived from. To count a visitor once per day rather than once per click, we compute a one-way hash of the date, the site, your IP address and your browser's user-agent string. Your IP address itself is never written down — only that hash, which is discarded after a short retention period.

Crash reports & anonymous usage data (the app)

The Hutch app sends two kinds of anonymous telemetry. Both are on by default, the welcome tour asks about them in its “Help improve Hutch” step, and each has its own switch, there and in Settings → General → Privacy. Switching one off takes effect immediately.

  • Anonymous crash reports, via Sentry (EU-hosted). When Hutch crashes or freezes for several seconds: a technical stack trace, the app and macOS versions, the Mac model, and a short trail of what Hutch was doing, like “Pounce opened” or “Settings: General”.
  • Anonymous usage counts, via PostHog (EU-hosted). Which tools are switched on, how often each is used and for what kind of action (a calculation, a left-half snap, a toggle), how far people get through the welcome tour, trial days left, when the licence screen appears, whether activation worked, and which version you updated from. Counts are added up on your Mac and sent in batches.

Both carry a random install ID that Hutch makes up on first launch, so a crash and a count from the same copy can be told apart. It isn't derived from your hardware, your account or your licence, and no profile of you is built from it. IP-based location lookup is switched off.

Never in a crash report or usage count:

  • File names, folders or paths
  • Links and web addresses you open or search
  • Clipboard contents
  • What you type in Pounce or anywhere else
  • Window titles or screen contents
  • Your licence key, email address or AI provider keys
  • Your name, your Mac's name or any hardware serial

Who else touches your data

  • Paddle or Creem — payment, invoicing, tax, and refunds, for orders each of them processes.
  • Google Cloud — hosts the licence records, in the EU.
  • Our email provider — delivers your licence key. It sees your email address and the message.
  • GitHub — serves this website and app downloads.
  • Sentry (EU) — receives anonymous crash reports from the app, while that switch is on.
  • PostHog (EU) — receives anonymous usage counts from the app, while that switch is on.
  • jsDelivr and Cloudflare — serve public currency rates when you convert a currency. They see an ordinary download.
  • ipify — answers when you ask Pounce for your public IP.

We don't sell anything to anyone, and there are no advertising or tracking networks involved.

How long we keep it

Licence records are kept for as long as the licence is valid, since a perpetual licence needs a record to stay perpetual. Website visit hashes expire automatically. Tax records are kept by the reseller that processed your order, for whatever period the law requires.

Your rights

You can ask what we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it — write to support@coursion.studio and we will answer within 30 days. Deleting your licence record means the licence can no longer be verified or recovered, so we'll confirm before doing it.

Children

Hutch isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect anything from them.

Changes

If this policy changes we'll update the date at the top. If a change materially affects what we collect, we'll say so in the app's release notes rather than quietly editing this page.

Who controls your data, and how to reach us

The data controller is Kuray Öğün, trading as Coursion Studio, of Ankara, Türkiye.

Email support@coursion.studio about anything on this page — including asking for a copy of what we hold, or asking us to delete it.

The reseller that processes your order — Paddle or Creem — acts as Merchant of Record and is a separate controller for the payment itself; their privacy policy covers the card details we never see.
